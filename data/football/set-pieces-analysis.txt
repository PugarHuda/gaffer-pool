Set-piece analysis
Date: 2026-05-28
Covers: Real Madrid, Manchester City

Why it matters: In tight big-matches (see the 2-2 in March), a single rehearsed
set piece can decide the tie. Both clubs treat dead balls as a repeatable,
high-value goal source.

Real Madrid:
- Attacking corners: near-post flick-on routine to a late-arriving midfielder at
  the penalty spot; Bellingham is a primary target on the second phase.
- Defensive corners: zonal with two man-markers on the biggest threats. Vulnerable
  to fast short-corner combinations that overload one side.
- Set-piece goals this season: 9 scored, 5 conceded.

Manchester City:
- Attacking corners: designed screens ("blockers") to free Haaland at the back post.
- Free-kicks in wide areas: whipped delivery to the six-yard box, second-ball
  structure to win knock-downs.
- Defensive corners: hybrid zonal + man; disciplined but occasionally beaten by
  runs attacking the goalkeeper's zone.
- Set-piece goals this season: 11 scored, 4 conceded.

Takeaway: City hold a small set-piece edge (more scored, fewer conceded). Against
either side, rehearsed routines and second-phase structure are worth the training
investment — dead balls may be the deciding margin.
