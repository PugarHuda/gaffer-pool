Tactical note: Breaking down a deep low block
Date: 2026-05-20
Author: Analyst desk

Problem: A team defending in a compact 5-4-1 or 4-5-1 low block concedes almost
no central space. Possession without penetration ("sterile domination") is the
common failure.

Principles to break it down:
1. Width and depth: pin the block wide with the wingers on the touchline and
   keep a striker on the last defender to stop the line stepping up.
2. Overload one side, switch to the other: draw the block across, then switch
   play fast to the isolated far winger for a 1v1.
3. Occupy the half-spaces: a runner between the fullback and centre-back is the
   hardest thing for a back five to pick up.
4. Third-man runs and cutbacks: get to the byline and cut back to the penalty
   spot; the highest-value zone against a deep block.
5. Set pieces become decisive: against a packed block, dead balls are often the
   most reliable goal source — invest in rehearsed routines.

Against City's positional attack, a low block should stay compact and accept
low possession, then counter into the space Rodri vacates. Against Madrid, a low
block must respect transition — do NOT commit numbers forward cheaply.
